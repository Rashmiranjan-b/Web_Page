About

I work in non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, at the meeting point of active matter, population genetics and fluid dynamics. My research combines stochastic lattice models, continuum theory and large-scale simulation to explain how spatial structure emerges from microscopic rules.

Recent work shows how diffusive noise governs the earliest stages of genetic demixing, and how motile populations form traveling waves, stripes and labyrinths. I am also applying machine learning to phase transitions in spin models, and building lattice Boltzmann methods for fluids with non-reciprocal interactions.

I write simulation codes in Fortran, C++ and Python.

Research

Traveling waves, phase separation and pattern formation in the active stepping stone model

Preprint, arXiv:2610.02346 (2026)

Bacteria and other microbes both reproduce and swim. We extend the classic stepping stone model of population genetics to two dimensions with motile individuals that carry an orientation, tumble between directions and migrate preferentially along it. Coupling growth with active motion produces a rich set of states: expanding rings, traveling fronts, wavy stripes, labyrinths and spot lattices. A continuum theory reproduces the patterns seen in the stochastic simulations.

Diffusive noise controls early stages of genetic demixing

Published in Physical Review Letters 137, 098401 (2026)

When two genetic variants share a habitat, random reproduction eventually lets one take over a region, a process called demixing. The standard picture attributes this to genetic drift alone. Using a one-dimensional stepping stone model, we show that at early times the dominant source of fluctuations is instead the random hopping of individuals between neighbouring demes. Fluctuations grow as √T in this regime before crossing over to linear growth, and data for different system sizes collapse onto one curve when time is scaled by L².

Multicomponent lattice Boltzmann method with non-reciprocal interactions

Ongoing

In active and living systems, one component can be attracted to another that is repelled in return, breaking Newton’s third law at the effective level. I am developing a multicomponent lattice Boltzmann scheme that includes such non-reciprocal couplings while keeping full hydrodynamics, to study how they reshape droplet formation, coarsening and flow-driven patterns.

Machine learning for spin models

Ongoing

Can a neural network find a phase transition without being told what an order parameter is? I train feed-forward networks on spin configurations of the Ising model and read off the critical temperature from where the network’s output changes. Finite-size scaling of the outputs across system sizes recovers the critical behaviour, and I am extending the approach to models where the order is less obvious.
